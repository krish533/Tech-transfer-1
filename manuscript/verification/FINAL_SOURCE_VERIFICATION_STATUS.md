# PCSI literature-source audit: what was independently checked

The manuscript contains **50 citation mentions and 32 cited bibliography items**. Every cited key has a BibTeX entry; none is unused. `FINAL_CURRENT_50_CITATION_REGISTER.csv` maps the **current** manuscript line and paragraph to the earlier source-evidence record for each occurrence, so it supersedes older line numbers and historical concerns recorded in prior audits.

## Evidence levels (do not conflate)

* **Source-consistent at relevant passages:** 33 occurrences. Relevant passages from papers supplied earlier were compared with the narrow claims. This does not certify every page of every paper.
* **Publisher-abstract supported:** 7 occurrences, concentrated in four sources. The final text makes only arguments supported by those abstracts or treats the sources as conceptual motivation; stronger assertions require full text.
* **Scope-qualified conceptual analogy:** 7 occurrences. The cited studies are from neighboring settings (not causal identification of the effect of university IP policy phrasing).
* **Historically flagged and corrected:** 3 occurrences. The current text distinguishes the methodological claims of Egami et al. and Battaglia et al.; historical verdicts must not be read as current errors.

## Four sources inspected again against publisher materials

1. Dorothy E. Smith (2001), *Texts and the ontology of organizations and institutions*, pp. 159–198, DOI 10.1080/10245280108523557. Publisher abstract supports the role of texts in mediating, coordinating, regulating, and authorizing organizational activity; it **does not** support a measured causal effect of IP-policy language on inventor behavior. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10245280108523557
2. Linda L. Putnam and François Cooren (2004), *Alternative Perspectives on the Role of Text and Agency in Constituting Organizations*, pp. 323–333, DOI 10.1177/1350508404041995. The journal abstract explains organizational text/agency; it does **not** test the PCSI empirical claims. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1350508404041995
3. Lauren B. Edelman (1992), *Legal Ambiguity and Symbolic Structures*, pp. 1531–1576, DOI 10.1086/229939. The journal abstract supports an organizational mediation of law through symbols and formal structures in workplace compliance; it does **not** demonstrate field-level IP-policy changes caused by Stanford v. Roche. https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/229939
4. Ulrike Grömping (2006), *Relative Importance for Linear Regression in R: The Package relaimpo*, pp. 1–27, DOI 10.18637/jss.v017.i01. The article abstract confirms averaging importance across regressor orderings; the PCSI decomposition is an **implementation in this study**, checked independently from the data, not a result reported by Grömping. https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v017i01

No page-by-page certification is claimed for these four full original articles. The remaining literature checks are also limited to retrieved sections unless page-anchored in the register. The present release cannot honestly guarantee every sentence on every page of all 32 cited publications has been examined; this is an explicit remaining boundary, not an omitted task claim.
